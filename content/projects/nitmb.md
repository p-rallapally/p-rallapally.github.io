(tldr; toured Chicago and learned how awesome and ubiquitous math can actually be)

**What it is** 

Development of algorithms for computing gap-size distributions in randomly generated 2D and 3D clusters, and how they can be applied to biological data (particularly neuron shapes and bacteria movement). 

**Context** 

Somehow, I had gotten into an applied math REU at Northwestern as a freshman with no experience and barely any coursework. Must've been a personality hire. 

**What I did**

- Adapted Hoshen-Kopelman (clustering) algorithm to work in three dimensions instead of two
- Calculated gaps between said clusters under various conditions and compared the distribtutions
- Attempted to adapt code to handle image inputs

**What was hard**

- Understanding the physics behind what I was doing, even at a simple level
- Familiarizing myself with Python beyond the bare basics (was completely unaware of arrays, runtime complexity, etc.)
- Complete autonomy
- Financial responsibility (spent a good portion of my summer stipend eating out)

**What I learned**

- What the research lifestyle is actually like
- Several points of improvement of my character and work ethic
- Rigor is a worthy investment
- The existence of network science (would prove to be influential later on)
